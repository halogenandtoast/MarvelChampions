# En Sabah Nur — Age of Apocalypse scenario 5

Sources: official MC45 *Age of Apocalypse* rulebook, pp.19–20; cards 45147–45163 and 45184–45186; Rules Reference v1.8. This scenario uses Apocalypse's three-sided villain cards, independently of the two-sided cards in scenario 3.

## Setup and stages

Use Apocalypse I and II for standard play, or II and III for expert play. Begin in Biomorph form. Reveal En Sabah Nur's Pyramid and deal each player a facedown encounter card. En Sabah Nur is required; Celestial Tech and Clan Akkaba are the recommended customizable modular sets alongside the selected Standard set.

Defeating a nonfinal stage reveals the next stage in Biomorph form at its new starting hit points. Because the title remains Apocalypse, keep its attachments, upgrades, status cards and counters (RR v1.8). Defeating the final stage wins. Completing the Pyramid advances to The Rise of Apocalypse. The latter reveals a Superpower card on entry and loses the game if completed.

## Three forms

Changing form is neither revealing nor defeating a villain. Preserve the villain's entity ID, current stage, remaining and maximum hit points, attachments, status cards, and an activation already underway. Change the printed stats, traits, and keywords to the new face. Gaining stalwart in Giant form discards stun and confusion cards as the usual stalwart rules require.

Only changing to a **different** form resolves that form's Forced Response. The response's amount equals the current stage number: one, two, or three. Biomorph deals that much indirect damage to every player and has overkill on its attacks. Cyberpath places that much threat on every scheme and has retaliate 1. Giant heals that much damage and has stalwart. Starting a game or revealing a new stage is not a form change.

The three form treacheries activate Apocalypse against the revealing player if he is already in their matching form. Otherwise, they change him to that form and place one power counter on the main scheme. Their boosts change his form **after the current activation**, without adding a power counter.

The corresponding defeated side schemes activate Apocalypse against the player who defeated them if he is already in that form. Otherwise they change his form and give him tough. Attribution includes an ally or event controlled by the defeating player.

Staggering Strength attaches to Apocalypse, changes him to Giant form, and adds two ATK. When he attacks, it stuns the attacked player and discards itself after that activation. Its boost consumes the actual boost card and reveals it after the current activation.

## Power counters

After villain-phase step one, the current main scheme gains one power counter. If it then has at least four, remove four and have the first player discard encounter cards until a Superpower is discarded and reveal that card. Reaching four through a form treachery does not independently trigger this check; the printed check is part of the villain-phase response.

## Celestial Tech

Celestial Armor interrupts the attached villain's scheme; Celestial Weapon interrupts its attack. Each discards the player's top deck card and checks its printed resource icons. A wild icon resolves every listed outcome. Multiple different nonwild symbols resolve each matching outcome once; repeated identical symbols do not multiply that outcome.

Armor's energy result heals the villain two, mental confuses the player and discards Armor, and physical gives the villain tough. Weapon's energy result deals two nonattack identity damage, mental discards one hand card, and physical stuns the player and discards Weapon. Nonattack damage and threat placement use the normal interrupt windows.

Celestial Tech resolves each Celestial attachment's corresponding effect against the revealing player. With no such attachment on the villain, search the encounter deck and discard pile for a Celestial attachment and reveal it, then shuffle.

## Clan Akkaba

Ancient Ritual is permanent and enters during setup with five threat. After a positive threat placement brings it to ten or more, remove five threat and deal each player a facedown encounter card. A single placement resolves this response once, even if ten threat remains afterwards; removing threat is not a new placement.

Ozymandias has toughness and villainous and places its scheming threat on Ancient Ritual. Scarab has quickstrike and adds one Ritual threat after attacking, or three instead if that attack defeated an ally. A defeated Clan Akkaba Zealot adds two Ritual threat; its boost adds one. Tyrant Worship adds five.

Ozymandias and Tyrant Worship's boosts offer three Ritual threat or three additional boost icons on that boost card. The icon choice modifies the actual icon count, rather than merely adding three ATK or SCH to the activation.

## Rulings

No new project-owner ruling has been required. Save/reload and undo preserve all three forms and card-local attack/boost state. Tests cover standard/expert setup, form retention and responses, stage progression, power counters, matching-form cards, Celestial resource branches, and Clan Akkaba threat and boost effects.
