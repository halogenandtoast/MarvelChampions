## Scenario setup and progression

Use Dark Beast I then II in standard mode; II then III in expert mode. The only main scheme is Dark Beast's Bogus Journey: initial threat 1 per player, escalation 1 per player, threshold 10 per player. Completing it loses the game.

Dark Beast, Blue Moon, Genosha, and Savage Land are required sets. Dystopian Nightmare is the recommended optional modular. Before normal Setup cards enter play, put all cards belonging to Blue Moon, Genosha, and Savage Land aside. Expert mode also reveals High-Tech Goggles.

Each villain stage's reveal chooses one remaining set-aside environment at random using the stored game generator. Reveal that physical environment, then shuffle the rest of its encounter set into the encounter deck. Stage II and III additionally deal one facedown encounter card to each player. Sets already recruited remain in the encounter deck; later stage reveals add an unused set. A newly revealed Setting discards every other Setting environment in play.

Dark Beast's attack interrupt resolves the current Setting's Special for the attacked player before the attack proceeds. A stunned attack does not initiate that interrupt. Villainous minion boost effects that attach High-Tech Goggles or Genetic Enhancement still attach them to Dark Beast, rather than the activating minion.

## Settings as standalone modulars

These sets can be used with other scenarios. Their Setup environments and all references to the current Setting therefore work independently of Dark Beast's scenario module.

| Setting | Constant effect | Special |
|---|---|---|
| The Savage Land | Villain gains retaliate 1 | Acting player discards three cards from their player deck |
| Genosha | Villain gains steady | Place one threat on the main scheme |
| Blue Area of the Moon | Every minion gains guard | Deal one damage to the acting identity |

The Special belongs to the Setting currently in play, including after a different environment replaces the set's own Setting. Thus Pterosaur can cause Genosha's threat placement or the Blue Moon's damage. Resolving it as a cost happens before discarding the attachment with that cost. Threat and non-attack damage retain their ordinary interrupt windows.

Land Out of Time has a narrower condition: it uses The Savage Land only when that environment is in play. Its indirect damage counts resource icons on the cards actually discarded by the Special, after discard resolution; a double-resource card contributes two and an ordinary wild icon contributes one. Domino's hero rule doubles her discarded wild icons. If Savage Land is absent, find and reveal it instead, without dealing that indirect damage.

## Timing and physical cards

- Time-Travel Shenanigans uses the current environment's encounter set, discarding until a matching card is found and revealing that exact card.
- Cruel Experiment reveals the discarded minion before attaching, so that minion's reveal abilities and entry restrictions resolve normally. It cannot attach to a unique minion that failed to enter play.
- Escaped Mutant moves between identities as its physical card. Magistrate attaches it without revealing; Police State reveals it. Removed-from-game cards and the victory display are excluded from Find.
- Armored Unibike's required engagement is decided before the engagement windows and quickstrike check. Escaped Mutant grants quickstrike only when a Genosha minion engages its attached identity.
- Teamwork is the existing Rules Reference keyword. An Imperial Guardsman attachment adds the trait, allowing other minions' Teamwork to include that minion; it does not grant Teamwork itself.
- Gladiator's immunity applies while Trial by Combat is in play. Trial's defeat shuffles only Imperial Guard minions in the encounter discard pile.
- Giant Ape and Magistrate use the player who defeated them, including attacks or abilities originating from an ally or another player's card.

## Campaign

The campaign's mission, Overseer, opening ally, and expert health rules are implemented by the shared campaign layer. This scenario uses the same mission setup and victory processing as the first three scenarios. It does not independently apply mission rewards or persistent campaign changes.

No additional project-owner ruling was needed; the official rulebook and RR v1.8 govern these mechanics.
