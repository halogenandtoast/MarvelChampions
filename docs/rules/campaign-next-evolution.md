# NeXt Evolution

Implementation reference for the [official rulebook](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-fantasyflightgames/uploads/2026/09/mc40_next_evolution_rulebook-web.pdf) (MC40), [campaign log](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-fantasyflightgames/uploads/2026/09/mc40_next_evolution_campaign_log-compressed.pdf), and Rules Reference v1.8. Printed text and [current errata](faq-errata.md) take precedence over MarvelCDB. The official rulebook was retrieved from FFG's current support page on 2026-10-31.

## General rules (pp3–7)

Player side schemes are player cards, played as actions, whose printed cost is paid normally. Their initial threat is their target threat; their When Defeated abilities resolve before they move to the victory display. They share a limit of one in solo/two-player games and two in three/four-player games. Exceeding the limit requires the first player to choose a player side scheme in play to discard. Campaign schemes explicitly ignore this limit. See current Rules Reference for thwarting restrictions and setup order.

Assault changes the stat used for a basic thwart to ATK. An ally also uses its attack consequential damage for that thwart. It remains a thwart, not an attack.

Costs marked per player multiply the number by the number of players who began the scenario. The same starting-player count applies after elimination.

An ally's defeat retains its actual discard or victory-display destination through interrupts. Regroup can replace only an allowed discard departure: protected Morlocks and allies with Victory retain their defeat destination. See RR v1.8 Victory X and the [FFG Regroup clarification, January 11, 2023](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/official-ffg-rulings/#232).

When an effect attacks an ally directly, undefended damage goes to that ally. Boost references to "you" refer to its controller. "When/while the enemy attacks you" abilities do not trigger for a direct attack against an ally. The newer Rules Reference exception for **after an enemy attacks you** does include a directly attacked ally controlled by that player (FFG clarification in the post-v1.7/v1.8 archive); this supersedes the older blanket rulebook wording for after-attack abilities. Any player may defend normally; overkill from a defeated ally goes to its controller's identity.

The Hope Summers encounter set may be added to any scenario and does not consume a modular-set slot. The encounter Hope (40130) is required for scenarios 3–5; she is controlled by the first player and is exempt from the ally limit. Her departure loses the game. The basic Hope (40204) is forbidden in NeXt Evolution campaign decks. Cards 40190–40203 are campaign-specific and may only enter decks when campaign instructions direct it.

Campaign order is Morlock Siege, On the Run, Juggernaut, Mister Sinister, Stryfe. Identities stay fixed; decks and aspects can change legally between games. Follow ordinary setup, then campaign setup in printed order. A loss retries without victory updates; expert Stryfe loss ends the campaign.

## Campaign schemes and persistent log

Choose one previously unchosen campaign player side scheme each scenario. Its paired encounter card joins the encounter deck for the rest of the campaign whether or not the scheme is defeated. Defeating the scheme puts its reverse environment into play. Victory marks environments still in play as earned, and all earned environments begin subsequent scenarios in play. An undefeated chosen scheme is permanently unavailable. On a retry after losing, choose the same scheme for that scenario; any reward must be earned again during the retry.

Environment counters replenish when environments enter a new game. Pouches and Safehouse are earned by environment actions during that game; the campaign does not permanently add them to player decks.

Expert victories record surviving identities' remaining HP capped at their base HP. Eliminated players skip victory steps and must pay the next scenario's full-heal penalty to rejoin. Between scenarios, identities begin at recorded HP and may pay that scenario's penalty to heal fully.

## Scenario 1: Morlock Siege (pp9–10)

Randomize all seven Marauders into a villain deck using A faces (B faces in expert). Only the top villain is in play. Each defeated villain moves under Routed, and the newly exposed villain activates against each player in order. Discard matching-title minions when a new villain enters. Defeat three villains to win. Expert Routed grants retaliate 1 per villain under it. When a matching-title minion is revealed while its villain is in play, discard it and reveal another encounter card.

Knock, Knock gains a knock counter after villain-phase step one. Its advancement after three knock counters differs from threat advancement: only the knock-counter path gives entering Morlocks tough. Mutant Massacre introduces one Morlock per player, two in solo, and loses if no Morlocks remain. Morlocks are exempt from ally limits and cannot leave play through card abilities; defeating them by damage is permitted.

Campaign setup chooses the first campaign scheme and shuffles its paired encounter card. Victory records the three defeated Marauder titles, Morlocks still in play, earned environment, and expert HP.

## Scenario 2: On the Run (p11)

Choose one random Marauder villain, remove the other villains and its matching minion, then attach confident Hope's Captor. The first defeat resets printed HP and flips the attachment instead; desperate Hope's Captor advances to stage 2 and grants extra HP. This is one villain identity, so statuses and other attachments remain.

Campaign setup removes recorded defeated Marauder villains BEFORE Gotta Get Away setup randomizes the remaining candidates; their matching minions remain. For each Morlock saved, choose a player to search their deck for one card into hand. Put the earned environment into play and, if there was one, give each enemy tough. Choose the next scheme and shuffle every chosen scheme's paired encounter card. Expert full heal costs one acceleration token. Victory records earned environments and expert HP.

## Scenario 3: Juggernaut (p14)

Momentum counters increase Juggernaut's ATK and carry between villain stages. Helmet removal/flipping uses the printed momentum requirements; do not clear momentum through ordinary villain advancement.

Campaign setup puts all earned environments into play in any chosen order, then adds one momentum per environment. Shuffle Black Tom Cassidy plus one Creeping Willow per starting player; deal one to each player facedown and shuffle the one remaining card into the encounter deck. This requires Black Tom's modular set. Choose the next campaign scheme and shuffle all chosen paired encounter cards. Expert full heal costs one facedown encounter card. Victory records Hope's damage, earned environments, and expert HP.

## Scenario 4: Mister Sinister (p16)

Set aside Flight, Super Strength, and Telepathy during setup, ignoring their Setup keywords for this scenario. Randomly remove one stage 2 and randomize the other two. Reveal Sinister Intent before Mister Sinister II in expert: advance to the first random stage 2, resolve both faces, then resolve the villain's When Revealed. Stage-2 reveals attach their Superpower and shuffle the remaining associated set into the encounter deck. The second stage 2 advances to Sinister Ends.

Campaign setup puts earned environments into play, puts Teleported Away into play, and adds one threat per starting player for each environment. Choose to place previously recorded Hope damage on Hope or that much threat on Teleported Away. Choose the next campaign scheme and shuffle all chosen paired encounters. Expert full heal costs an acceleration token. Victory records Hope's damage, earned environments, and expert HP.

## Scenario 5: Stryfe (p18)

Most common type counts actual cards of each type in hand; tied types have the same count. Stryfe's constant ATK bonus recalculates whenever the hand changes until attack damage is determined. Spending a prevention event after that point does not recalculate already determined damage. Stryfe's Grasp flipping preserves its remaining threat; Living Bomb prevents Stryfe's defeat until removed.

Campaign setup puts earned environments into play and adds one threat per starting player to Stryfe's Grasp per environment. Choose whether to place recorded Hope damage on Hope or that much threat on the Grasp. In player order, discard encounter cards until a minion or Psionic attachment is discarded and reveal it; afterwards shuffle encounter discard into deck. Choose the final campaign scheme and shuffle all chosen paired encounters. Expert full heal costs one facedown encounter card; an expert loss loses the campaign. Victory wins the campaign.

## Current official clarifications

Rules Reference v1.8 p64: Malice remains a minion after attaching to the possessed ally. She retains damage; may be targeted and attacked; cannot be defeated again, even if healed or given HP; is unengaged and cannot activate; leaves play when the card she is attached to leaves play. The possessed ally becomes an engaged, blank-text minion with original traits plus Possessed and SCH equal to THW. Treat Malice and that possessed minion as separate game elements.

Rules Reference v1.8 p69: Inhibitor Collar's permission for any player to use its removal action is rules text.

Additional FFG answers reproduced in the [post-v1.6 rulings archive](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-6/) clarify Assault: a dash THW does not prevent a character with numeric ATK from thwarting an Assault scheme (2025-06-17), and THW bonuses do not increase its threat removal (2025-11-14). For Hidden in the Clutter, the later 2024-12-10/2025-01-31 answers resolve its enemy attack before discarding the attachment, overriding the earlier 2024-02-15 sequence. An enemy activation can interrupt a hero action; the rule deferring nested activations applies to enemy activations.

Pushed to the Limit cannot be initiated while the villain is stunned, because its attack cost cannot be paid (FFG, 2023-10-12). The stun must be removed another way.

## Hope Summers in alter-ego form (rules interpretation, 2026-11-01)

Hope Summers has 0 ATK and 0 THW while her controller is in alter-ego form. This is an interpretation derived from the current Rules Reference, not a direct FFG ruling or a project-owner ruling. A search of the official expansion rulebook and the available official-answer archives, including the latest archive through 2026-01-17, found no answer addressing this case directly.

The [Rules Reference v1.8](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-fantasyflightgames/uploads/2026/09/mc_rulesreference_v18_compressed.pdf) **Identity** entry (p.23) places the inactive hero side out of play. Hope's printed star values are defined by her controller's hero's ATK and THW; while that hero is out of play, those values are undefined. **Star Icon** (p.40) and **Non-Numerical Variable** (p.30) treat an undefined star or non-numerical value as 0. The relevant local transcriptions are [Identity and Non-Numerical Variable](glossary-i-o.md) and [Star Icon](glossary-s-z.md). Recalculate Hope's values when her controller changes form; apply other modifiers to Hope normally.

## Rulings (from the project owner, 2026-11-01)

- Hidden in the Clutter follows the newer official timing: resolve the resulting enemy attack and its responses before discarding the attachment. Damage dealt back during that attack can trigger Hidden in the Clutter again and cause repeated attacks. Do not suppress these triggers with an invented limit.
- A confused Hulk ally requires a legal Assault target before attempting that basic thwart to clear confusion. The owner selected this requirement after searches found no explicit official answer for the targetless dash-THW edge case.

## Giant Wasp and Assault (rules interpretation, 2026-11-01)

The first allocation target of Giant Wasp's divided basic thwart determines one shared stat pool: effective Assault uses her modified ATK, while an ordinary initial target uses modified THW. That pool can be divided among legal Assault and ordinary schemes; later allocations do not change or add another pool. The existing first positive allocation identifies the initial target, without adding a separate minimum-allocation rule. A numeric zero pool resolves without removing threat.

This is an interpretation from an official analogy, not a direct FFG ruling on Wasp and Assault. In the [official March 9, 2023 Wasp/Befuddle answer](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/official-ffg-rulings/), Alex explains that Wasp initially chooses one attack target; if Befuddle substitutes THW, she can divide that single THW pool normally among targets without double dipping. Substituting ATK for a divided thwart follows the same approach. Fresh searches of the current and older official-answer archives found no direct Wasp/Assault mixed-target answer. Assault and stat modifiers otherwise follow Rules Reference v1.8: ATK modifiers affect an ATK-selected pool, THW modifiers do not, and blanked Assault does not substitute ATK.
