# Cable — NeXt Evolution

Source: [FFG NeXt Evolution rulebook](https://images-cdn.fantasyflightgames.com/filer_public/73/e2/73e23a2b-3f2a-4173-8fee-cf0073ab11c8/mc40_next_evolution_rulebook-web.pdf), pages 2–3, 21; Rules Reference v1.8, Player Side Scheme, Player Side Scheme Limit, Victory Display, Moving, You. Rules below are paraphrased. RR v1.8 supersedes earlier rules where they conflict.

## Player side schemes

Player side schemes are player cards that enter the villain's play area alongside the main scheme. Pay their printed cost to play them during your own turn. They enter with their starting threat, multiplied by the number of starting players where printed. Heroes and allies can thwart them. Abilities referring to schemes or side schemes include them.

The shared limit is one with one or two starting players, two with three or four. A new player side scheme may enter while already at the limit. The first player chooses schemes to discard until the limit is met. This is not defeat and does not trigger When Defeated. Campaign schemes in this expansion explicitly do not count against that limit.

A player side scheme defeated with victory X goes to the shared victory display. A player side scheme discarded for another reason goes to its owner's discard pile. Preserve its owner and physical card ID when moving it between zones.

## Cable

Nathan Summers may include player side schemes of any aspect. Soldier X searches his deck and discard pile for a player side scheme and puts it into play during setup. His signature card count is 15, and the printed Leadership starter deck has 40 player cards. Technovirus Resurgence and the five nemesis cards are separate encounter cards.

Cable's response is once each phase, and requires Cable to defeat the side scheme. His own events and unattached upgrades extend his identity. His allies and supports are distinct cards; their defeats do not ready Cable. A confused thwart attempt does not defeat a scheme.

Technovirus Purge grants its named bonus while it is in the victory display. The modifier disappears if that card returns to play. Both identity forms gain Psionic; only the hero gains +1 THW, ATK and DEF. Only side schemes count toward Cable's scaling abilities; Forced Amnesia itself is an upgrade despite also entering victory.

Temporal Leap returns a side scheme from victory as part of its cost, with its normal starting threat. It then moves four existing threat from the impending main scheme to that side scheme, or as much as exists. Moving is not placement, removal, or thwarting. The pending new threat then resolves and may still complete the main scheme.

## Official guidance checked

The official rulebook FAQ states that E.V.A. is immediately discarded when Fantomex is absent. When Technovirus Resurgence puts Purge into play over the player side scheme limit, resolve the first player's discard choice before attaching the obligation. If they discard Purge, the obligation cannot attach and instead deals its owner an encounter card.

The following designer answers are archived at [Hall of Heroes](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-5/), with the responder/date supplied there. Searches also checked the current Rules Reference and newer guidance before applying these answers:

- Alex Werner, August 10, 2023, answer 62: Cable's hero-form events and upgrades can remove threat from Technovirus Purge. Blanking Purge blanks its restriction and victory keyword; defeating it while blank sends it to discard.
- Alex Werner, August 31, 2023, answer 78: supports belonging to any player can remove threat from Purge because they are not characters or identity extensions.
- Alex Werner, August 31, 2023, answer 79: Deathlok checks the upgrade's attachment requirements and limits, and ignores play restrictions. A friendly-character upgrade such as Honorary Avenger may attach even if the playing identity is not an Avenger. Sky Cycle requires Deathlok to be an Avenger; Training requires him to be X-Men and comply with its limit.
- Alex Werner, May 10, 2024, answer 182: Temporal Leap resolves before the threat that would complete the main scheme is placed. Its movement may be insufficient; after the pending placement, the players may still lose.

No project-owner ruling was requested or substituted for an official answer.

Frenemies chooses both different scheme targets from the initially legal schemes before either removal resolves, and removes threat from them simultaneously. A crisis scheme cannot be cleared first to make the main scheme a legal second target. If only one scheme is initially legal, the effect removes threat from that scheme.
